### Slide 2: Marine Life & Ecosystem Overview
**Marine Life**  
Marine life refers to all the plants, animals, algae, fungi, bacteria, and other organisms that live in the saline (saltwater) habitats of oceans, seas, or the brackish water of coastal estuaries, wetlands, and lagoons.

- The oceans provide **50% of the world’s oxygen supply** and they provide essential ecosystem services that allow our planet to function in a healthy way.
- Ocean currents govern our weather systems; if they were to be interrupted, the extreme weather consequences would be drastic.
- The oceans can only provide these services by maintaining delicately balanced ecosystems, made up of food chains and biological systems.
- Human activity risks the shutdown of essential ecosystem services that we rely on to survive.

### Slide 3: The Problem
**The Problem**  
Humans continue to pollute the oceans, despite the huge risks to marine life and to ourselves. Marine pollution comes in many forms such as plastic pollution, acidification, and oil spills.

- **Commercial Fishing & Trawling:** The largest risk posed to marine life is the fishing industry. Vast commercial fleets trawling the oceans has been common practice for many years and sadly shows no signs of slowing.
- **Aquaculture Pollution:** Fish farms pollute waters with the chemicals needed to treat diseases caused by the appalling conditions, excess feed, and concentrated fish waste. This pollution causes algal blooms, resulting in the deaths of wild fish, too.
- **Plastic Waste:** Plastic pollution kills 100 million marine creatures every year. This could cause endangerment of species which directly affects the entire global ecosystem, including humans.

### Slide 4: Existing Solutions and Their Limitations
**Existing Solutions**  
- **Autonomous River-Cleaning Devices:** These already exist but consume extensive space, energy, and capital for operation, making them difficult to deploy across diverse water bodies.
- **Pollution Monitoring Systems:** Typically rely on stationary, fixed sensors monitoring a single static point. Consequently, hundreds of sensors must be installed to survey even a modest water body.
- **Water Treatment Centers:** The majority of water bodies in India remain untreated and exposed to industrial and urban effluents. While large treatment plants exist, they cannot be deployed universally.

**Identified Major Drawbacks:**
1. **Lack of Mobility:** Confined to localized, fixed geographic points.
2. **Prohibitive Cost:** Unaffordable for local communities and everyday stakeholders.
3. **Bulky Form Factor:** Too large to maneuver inside smaller rivers, lagoons, and estuaries.

### Slide 5: Our Solution — AquaPulse
**Our Solution: AquaPulse**  
An autonomous (self-operated) submarine designed to monitor pollution levels, aquatic life, their movements, and their behavior.

- **Compact & Agile:** Engineered with a compact form factor small enough to navigate and monitor shallow water bodies, coastal lagoons, and estuaries.
- **Dynamic Mobility:** Because it is mobile, it is not confined to a single station. It can reposition across multiple maritime zones dynamically, adapting to changing environmental needs without physical boundaries.
- **Economical & Accessible:** Low fabrication and operational cost makes it accessible to local fisherfolk, researchers, and coastal environmental bodies.

### Slide 6: Autonomous Architecture & Intelligence
**Autonomous Operations**  
Our submarine is completely autonomous, requiring zero continuous human intervention during missions:

- **Sensor-Driven AI Models:** Onboard sensors relay live oceanographic and optical inputs into machine learning models.
- **Autonomous Decision Engine:** Based on analyzed metrics, the submarine autonomously makes mission decisions—such as shadowing a specific school of fish, relocating to an unmapped sector, or issuing urgent alerts to coastal operators.
- **Actionable Intelligence:** Balances full operational autonomy with delivering high-fidelity data intelligence to human decision-makers.

### Slide 7: Energy Source — Solar Power Harvesting
**Energy Source: Solar**  
The submarine is powered by solar energy, eliminating the requirement for manual battery replacements:

- **Autonomous Surface Charging:** Once deployed, the submarine operates independently. When not actively executing submerged missions, the AI schedules surfacing cycles during peak daylight hours to replenish its energy storage.
- **24/7 Continuous Mission Capability:** Ensures 24/7 uptime without physical shore tethering.
- **Overcast & Night Redundancy:** Internal high-capacity rechargeable battery banks maintain full subsea operations during overcast conditions or overnight missions, resuming charging upon the return of solar irradiance.
- *(Analogous to Mars rover energy management paradigms).*
